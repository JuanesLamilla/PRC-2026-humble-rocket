    The final submission¶

    This document describes the model behind humble-rocket_final.parquet: predictions for the 670,790 departures of final_ranking.parquet (January, February, June and July 2026). It sits on top of the stack documented in docs/01-docs/05: the members here are trained on a compact matrix of raw-derived features plus the ADS-B ground-track columns, and the class model additionally consumes the team stack's prediction (docs/02-method.md) as one of its experts. pipeline/RUNS.md records every training run; final/ holds the blend and assembly code.

    Problem¶

    Predict the taxi-out time (take-off minus off-block, seconds) of each 2026 departure at ten European airports, trained on twelve labelled months of 2025. Scoring is RMSE. February and June were never on the daily leaderboard; the arrivals of all four months are labelled, the departures are not.

    What the data taught us¶

    Three properties of the data shape the design.

    The join window. The organizers attached an NM flight plan to a departure only when the airport's off-block stamp lay within 3,606 s of the NM system's last off-block time (LOBT). We verified this on all 2,062,577 NM departures of 2025: max |BLOCK − LOBT| is exactly 3,606 s. Two consequences follow.

    1. On every NM row the label provably lies in [MVT − LOBT − 3606, MVT − LOBT + 3606]. Projecting a prediction onto that interval can only reduce its error, so the window clip is the last step of the pipeline, and the strongest members build the bound into their architecture.
    2. "No flight plan" is partly a label-selected event: the join failed because the stamp was far from LOBT. The no-NM lane (about 1 % of rows, roughly a third of the squared error) has a different label distribution and gets its own specialists and blend cells.

    Schedule defaults. A recording artifact sets the off-block stamp equal to the scheduled time on a few percent of rows, which makes the label exactly C = MVT − SCHED. Whether a row is a default is driven by operator recording practice, which arrivals reveal without touching any departure label. Most members model P(default) explicitly and predict a mixture.

    Lane structure. Error concentrates in a few row populations: no-NM rows, NM rows with long delays, and LIRF. Members are combined per lane rather than globally.

    Members¶

    Trained by the scripts in pipeline/, thirteen folds each: folds 1-12 hold out one 2025 month (the member's honest out-of-fold prediction of it), fold 0 trains on all of 2025 and predicts the evaluation rows.

    • Two-stage GBM (gbm_nested.py): stage 1 predicts properties of the off-block stamp (the bounded offset d = BLOCK − LOBT and its quantiles, P(default), P(long)) with inner month-held folds, so no stage-2 training row sees an in-fold stage-1 fit. Stage 2 fits the taxi time, including the mixture y_mix = p_def · C + (1 − p_def) · normal-model. Variants: a CatBoost mixture, a per-airport mixture, and a four-class stamp specialist (normal / default / near-default / long) that sees the other members' disagreement as features and combines class-specific value models by expectation.
    • Distributional FT-transformer (ftdist.py): a feature-token transformer whose head is a softmax over taxi-time bins masked to the NM join window, plus a point mass at y = C masked where a default is infeasible. The prediction is the distribution mean. Variants: base (7 seeds), stage-1 tokens, strike-calendar tokens, EOBT-free (18 seeds), no-NM rows upweighted.
    • Class-structured combiner (class_mix.py), the strongest single member: y = p_def · C + p_near · (C − offset) + p_long · v_long + p_normal · Σ g_j n_j, where the class probabilities, the gate g over the members' conditional normal means n_j, and small corrections come from one MLP that sees every member's class signals (including the team stack's prediction). A second instance is trained on the no-NM rows only.

    Two member generations¶

    The challenge released its evaluation data twice: ranking.parquet (the January/July rows scored by the daily leaderboard) first, and final_ranking.parquet (all four months; the same January/July flights with the take-off stamp dithered by a few seconds) close to the deadline. That timing leaves two member generations:

    • the primary members above, whose fold 0 predicts the January/July rows. Each produced its thirteen folds in one nested run, so its out-of-fold behaviour is a faithful proxy for its fold-0 behaviour;
    • fold-0 retrains on the final matrix for the months the primary members never predicted (February/June).

    The retrains are one-off fold-0 fits, and that costs them something the 2025 validation cannot see: a stacked model's test-time inputs must come from the same model instances that generated its training inputs. Two retrained members break that symmetry on the final rows: the anomaly specialist's member-deviation features exist only for the January/July rows (its February/ June means collapse by ~500 s against every sibling member), and the class model consumed it as an expert (a 35-65 s depression against its siblings on exactly those two months, within ±10 s elsewhere). Both are therefore excluded from the February/June member lists; everything else about the two generations is identical. January/July rows are predicted by the primary members (their prediction maps to the re-released rows by adding the published take-off-stamp shift, which moves the label by exactly that amount).

    Blend¶

    Per lane (NM C < 1 h, NM C ≥ 1 h, no-NM LIRF, no-NM elsewhere): non-negative least squares on the simplex, lightly shrunk toward equal weights, fitted on the members' month-held 2025 out-of-fold predictions (final/blend.py). Weights scored on month k are fitted on the other eleven, so the February and June rows of the 2025 read are leave-that-month-out estimates, the honest stand-in for the never-evaluated months. On the NM lanes the class combiner carries most of the weight (0.98 / 0.82); the no-NM lanes are led by the transformer variants, and LIRF no-NM by the no-NM class specialist (0.75).

    Corrections (final/assemble.py)¶

    • Feeder lateness. When the stand's feeding arrival went in-block more than 90 min behind schedule, the blend overshoots slightly (the aircraft tends to leave quickly once ready). Offsets per C band are fitted on the 2025 out-of-fold blend with a heavy smoothing prior and validated month-held (better in 8 of 12 months, interval below zero). Arrival block times are published for the submission months, so the trigger is computable on every 2026 row.
    • LFPG EOBT drift. In June and July 2026 the filed EOBT at LFPG sits about four minutes later relative to LOBT than in any 2025 month; no other airport-month moves comparably. This is a filing-practice change no 2025-trained model has seen, and EOBT-anchored features mis-read exactly those rows. The EOBT-free transformer is immune by construction, so the mean gap between it and the base transformer over the affected cell (LFPG, NM, C < 1 h) estimates the induced bias and is added back there: +17 s in July, +16 s in June. Label-free: the drift is visible in published feature columns, and the magnitude comes from comparing two of our own models.

    Rules (label-free, proven on 2025)¶

    1. window clip (a projection, so it can only help);
    2. rollover identity: when MVT − LOBT > 20 h, LOBT kept the previous day's date and the label equals MVT − LOBT itself;
    3. twin cap: a no-NM departure whose arrival "twin" (same callsign and route, landing at another challenge airport within six hours) carries an NM plan must have fallen outside the join window, so y ≤ MVT − twin LOBT − 3606. On 2025, labels respect this bound on 810 of 812 twin rows with margin ≥ 300 s;
    4. floor at zero, round, Int32.

    Validation and results¶

    Everything is selected on 2025 with months held out; candidate changes were accepted on day-block bootstrap intervals of the paired RMSE delta together with counts of months improved, never on a single pooled number (the protocol of docs/03-validation.md). Rollover rows are excluded from member training and reported separately, because three or four rows a year would otherwise dominate any comparison.

    2025 month-held reads of the delivered construction, ex-rollover (results/blend_report.txt):

    read RMSE (s)
    all twelve months 191.09
    lane NM C < 1 h 149.37
    lane NM C ≥ 1 h 310.58
    lane no-NM other 588.53
    lane no-NM LIRF 2538.10
    February (leave-out, retrain member list) 200.87
    June (leave-out, retrain member list) 201.78

    The daily leaderboard (January/July 2026 only) scored this file's January/July half at 221.68. The leaderboard was used as a deployment sanity check of completed submissions; the pipeline incorporates no leaderboard-derived values, and its one 2026-specific term (the LFPG adjustment) is estimated label-free as described above.
